# Concord v3: claim and lease options

**Research stage 1c; evidence cutoff 2026-10-15.** This is an option report, not a policy change. `STRUCTURAL (source date: …)` means a mechanism or measurement limit holds independently of agent skill. `CAPABILITY-DEPENDENT (source date: …; re-test with current agents)` means an observed agent behavior could change with models, prompts, and tools. An undated source carries its 2026-10-15 local read date. Each evidence citation names the supplied input file and its source title. A source marked NOT READ within the notes supplies no evidence.

## 0. V2 baseline, decisions and delivery

The standing record is **D-UNIT-1-EXCLUSIVE**: a lease is an exclusive write lock on its unit; shared/hybrid modes wait for a land-time merge step (Q-UNIT-4; **D-UNIT-4-STRUCT-MERGE**). Under **D-LEASE-BOTTLENECK-ASAP** and shipped spec 181, that exclusion lasts only through first publish. Its retained landing hold and fence still authorize queue admission, while new writers may claim the same unit. **D-LEASE-5MIN-EXTEND** superseded the two-hour default direction of **D-LEASE-LONG-TTL**: five minutes is default, extension must be reliable, and long holds are exceptions. **D-LEASE-Q5-CPLUS** approves declare/grow/verify as a plan, not proof that its linked CLI, wait and pre-push contracts landed. **D-LEASE-WAIT-LINE** approves FIFO and human controls; **D-LEASE-QUEUE-AUTO** approves automatic *enqueuing* of refusals, not automatic *granting*. The owner's hybrid, issue, symbol and AI-rule ideas remain hypotheses under **D-LOCK-RESEARCH-SCOPE**; **D-RESEARCH-PHASE** requires approval of which tests run. Sources: docs/decisions.md, “Owner decisions — Concord” (2026-10-12–15); v2-history/v2-lease-decisions.md, “V2 lease decisions” (2026-10-11–15); s6-v2-claim-history.md, “Concord v2 claim and lease history for v3” (2026-10-15). “Shared by default” is open in concord-v3-architecture.html, draft 4 (2026-10-15).

“Approved” records owner intent, “specified” a design, and “shipped” behavior checked in the supplied 2026-10-15 source snapshot; these are different states (s6-v2-claim-history.md, “What landed, what did not”; v2-history/v2-lease-decisions.md).

The owner's mid-run API question has a concrete answer: **v2 planned one**. Spec 69's concord claim would add leases to the same intent during work, optionally wait in spec 72's FIFO, and spec 127 would first grow the server-authorized symbol start scope. It never became the complete CLI/MCP workflow: #1 concord start was not on main after repeated repair and lease livelock; only wait spec 72 launched and failed to land; 69/70/71/73 were parked behind it; 116 later replaced 72's grant design. Lower-level lease claiming exists, but does not fulfill that contract. **Proposed v3 deviation:** retain explicit, all-or-nothing mid-run scope growth for discovered exclusive units and exact-diff reconciliation, while comparing it with no-grow and advisory discovery before selecting a policy. C+ itself was start declaration, growth through fair waiting with deadlock refusal, then diff-derived claim of missing and release of unused units at publish/land. This recommendation is research, not a shipped claim (v2-history/69-concord-claim.md; 70-start-units.md; 71-publish-autoclaim.md; 127-sym-grow-92.md; s6-v2-claim-history.md, 2026-10-15).

**Wait designs differ.** Spec 72's release/expiry handler would grant the lease. Spec 116 instead gives a bounded, idempotent all-or-nothing request a 45-second priority *turn* within a five-minute maximum wait, requiring a new authenticated claim for a fence. V2's separate token catalog and repository Durable Objects could not synchronously recheck revocation in the release transaction. A proposed single-server v3 can reconsider auto-grant only with atomic grant-time authentication, revocation ordering and fencing. Both designs need cancellation, expiry, multi-unit aliases and parent-child overlap, no barging, privacy, caps and idempotent retries; 116's old active/reserved blocker rule also needs revision for 181's nonblocking landing holds (v2-history/72-lease-wait-queue.md; 73-lease-deadlock.md; 116-lease-wait-v2.md; 181-published-leases-stop-blocking.md, 2026-10-12–14).

**Measured v2 costs, distinct from replays.** The v3 draft reports 2,306 of 2,382 claims refused on Oct 13 and about five times more rounds under strict file exclusion in gen-v-research-tools: one historical episode and one repository, with no counterfactual. The draft's 2,382 denominator is not reconciled with the renewal spike's 2,392 grant events and 2,306 refusal events, so it must not be read as a comparable acceptance rate. Spec 181 records an hour without a queue land and mutual wait between changes 136/138. The lease-renewal spike's 10,801-event log found 128 expiries, 101 within ten minutes of renewal; 75/121 inspections exceeded five minutes; review waits were 3–218 minutes (median 37); 18 serial renewals took about 31 seconds, and a sidecar renewal invalidated land fences. The issue spike records #1's six attempts and 14 refusals against ten chains. These motivate phase-specific wait, renewal and repair measurement. S5 launcher runs held no Concord leases; their timings cannot measure actual lease wait (concord-v3-architecture.html, draft §3/§9, 2026-10-15; v2-history/181-published-leases-stop-blocking.md; lease-renewal-research.html; issue-claim-research.md, 2026-10-13–15).

### A. Lease kinds and lifecycle

| Option | How it works and cost | Failure mode, evidence, test |

A presence holder can **request an atomic upgrade** before its first protected edit, before a destructive operation, or after repeated measured conflict. The server checks policy, current exclusive writing holder and fence, grants a fresh fence or queues the request; presence alone gives no place ahead of existing FIFO waiters. Other presence holders can still edit optimistically, but their publish to that now-exclusive unit must be refused unless they acquire a valid fence or policy permits optimism. Upgrading after writing may waste work (**CAPABILITY-DEPENDENT, source date: 2026-10-15; re-test with current agents**), so nonmergeable assets need exclusion **before edit**. At first publish, convert the writing hold to a revision-bound nonblocking landing hold as in 181; at cancel release it. An expired holder must re-read and rebase. These are proposed protocol rules inferred from Chubby fencing, Git LFS verification and Gray conversion ordering (`s1-leases-lockservices-notes.md`, “The Chubby lock service,” 2006; `sp-f1-locks-notes.md`, “Git LFS File Locking API,” read 2026-10-15; `r11-lock-papers-notes.md`, “Granularity of Locks and Degrees of Consistency,” 1976). **STRUCTURAL (source dates: 1976, 2006, 2026-10-15 read):** a claim without downstream verification cannot enforce exclusion.

### C. Issue-declared leases and ready-set scheduling

The v3 draft already has an issue expected-units field (concord-v3-architecture.html, §6, 2026-10-15). Treat it as a **versioned prediction**, with source, confidence, policy version and reason. It neither assigns a worker nor grants source authority. V2 spec 70 proposed using the issue's validated units list for an all-or-nothing start; specs 69/127 then grow the same change during work; spec 71 reconciles actual diff units before push. **Proposed v3 extensions, separate from C+:** visible presence, issue work ownership, scheduling by predicted units, and optional presence-to-exclusive upgrade. Do not bundle these with initial declaration or call them shipped. Discovery of a missing unit triggers a versioned grow request: validate path protection and canonical aliases, atomically extend authorized scope and claim the missing exclusive units, or record refusal/queue and leave prior scope/fences usable. A policy refusal emits an event and stops protected publication; it is not a routine mid-run request for owner approval (D-FORGE-ISSUES; D-AGENT-FIRST-HUMAN-CONTROLS). At publish, derive actual units from the diff, claim missing and release unused before push, and refuse an unclaimed protected diff. This separates a recoverable declaration miss from a safety failure (v2-history/69-concord-claim.md; 70-start-units.md; 71-publish-autoclaim.md; 127-sym-grow-92.md, 2026-10-12–14).

**Issue ownership remains open under D-ISSUE-CLAIM-SPIKE.** Bounded choices are assignment only (visible, no expiry or exclusion), a separate renewable issue work lease (one active holder, fence and expiry), or both; a status field alone is only a signal. A run/session identity distinguishes agents sharing a principal token; stable issue intent survives change replacement, with attempt/change IDs linked as children. Under an issue-first start, success means issue lease plus **all** required units acquired, or a bounded resumable reservation; on unit failure roll back the issue lease or expire the reservation. Partial unit grants while retaining the issue can deadlock or hoard. Issue text changes need versioned edit/notify or selective freeze; close must revoke or refuse with an event, and reopen must not silently reinstate a stale holder. Expiry, disconnect and reassignment require fencing, audit and an explicit duplicate-work recovery path. These are alternatives, not approved choices; Q-ISSUE-CLAIM.1–5 remain owner decisions (v2-history/issue-claim-research.md, “Spike: what claiming an issue means for agent swarms”; issue-leases-research.html, “Issue Claims Research,” 2026-10-13). Agent Mail already attaches an issue ID as reservation reason at task start, providing advisory issue-to-path prior art (s2b-agent-mail-notes-part1.md, “MCP Agent Mail,” read 2026-10-15).

**S5 is scope pressure, not lease benefit.** Of 10,513 eligible runs, only 5,367 had a locally diffable commit; 4,752 lacked a SHA and 394 had no local diff. Among diffable runs, 19,814/26,494 declared file entries changed (74.8%). ### E. Contention and liveness

The observed-v2 baseline is **refuse and retry while another change is writing, then nonblocking landing holds after first publish** (181), with no durable FIFO. A separate decided-but-unbuilt C+ baseline adds an all-or-nothing grow request, FIFO and deadlock refusal; neither should be labeled current. An exclusive claim must be checked against unpublished writing holds, and publish/queue/land must check the claimant's **own** fence and revision-bound verified footprint. A published holder's landing authorization is retained or explicitly reacquired for each revision; release at submit without a replacement authorization would break landing. A repair must rederive changed units, reacquire or validate own fences, rebase on current main, republish and repeat inspection/review. A post-edit upgrade cannot prove the edit used current content: require a base check/rebase before accepting it, and account for discarded work. **CAPABILITY-DEPENDENT (source date: 2026-10-15; re-test with current agents):** wasted-work and restart cost vary by agent. Under an exclusive upgrade, optimistic writers can continue locally, but their publish for that protected unit must wait for a valid fence or be refused; there is no “exclusive” guarantee if both may publish.
